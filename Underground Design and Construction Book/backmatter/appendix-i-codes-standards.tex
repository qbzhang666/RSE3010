\chapter{Codes, Standards and Guidance}
\label{app:codes}

No single code governs the design of a tunnel (Section~\ref{sec:limit-state-design-and-codes}),
and the design basis of a project states which document governs each check.
Table~\ref{tab:codes-map} lists, for the subject of each part of this book,
the documents most often adopted for that purpose: the second-generation
Eurocodes, the international guidance of the tunnelling and concrete
associations, and the Australian standards. It records the editions current in
November 2026. Several of the Australian standards listed are under revision,
and the edition in force on a project is the one its technical requirements
name.

Two rules govern the use of the table. A document applies to a check only
where its scope covers that check: the Eurocodes, for instance, do not cover
underground structures as such, and their geotechnical and concrete parts are
applied to the components of a tunnel that fall within their scope. And where
two documents give different values for the same quantity, as the vibration
standards do (Table~\ref{tab:vibration-limits}), the one adopted is named
with the value, because the value alone does not say which framework it
belongs to.
